# Evidence-based resume writing

Use the selected evidence IDs and role positioning. Draft achievements before the summary. Each useful bullet communicates the user's action, a concrete problem or technical mechanism, and the outcome or scope. Order these naturally; do not force a metric into every sentence. Prefer specific qualitative outcomes to invented numbers.

Preserve metric meaning: product availability is not active usage; adoption is not causation; estimated savings are not measured results; potential savings are not realized savings. A product-wide result may provide context if attributed clearly, but cannot automatically be claimed as the user's result. Keep absolute numbers and percentages tied to their original population and window. Do not multiply assumptions into a dramatic aggregate.

Translate internal names into a short understandable purpose. Mention relevant technology in the context of actual work. Use direct verbs that accurately reflect ownership; distinguish designed, implemented, contributed, coordinated, and managed. Avoid adjectives such as visionary, world-class, and expert without evidence. Avoid generic claims about passion or teamwork.

Use readable sections, reverse chronological work experience, visible promotions, and a concise relevant skills section. Give high-value recent work sufficient space. Keep education brief for experienced engineers. Choose one or two pages according to the evidence, preserving comfortable typography. Do not shrink text just to force one page. A long master record is never the default application artifact.

Draft each target separately with a role-specific headline, evidence selection, and summary if it adds concrete value. Build the summary from the final strongest achievements. Do not blend unrelated targets into a keyword-heavy resume; honor a user-requested combined version when the evidence and role overlap support it. A staff-focused version needs scope and cross-team influence demonstrated in its bullets.

Keep an internal bullet-to-evidence map outside the submitted resume. In provisional review text, mark uncertain material clearly. In application-ready files, resolve or omit it; do not leave bracketed questions or uncertain numbers. Match the user's English voice and keep every claim explainable in an interview.

Return separate drafts, the evidence map, and only the factual questions that prevent finalization. Do not let stylistic polishing hide missing substance.

When a user requires identical skills across variants, keep a canonical skills block and compare labels, order and content after edits. Propagate factual corrections and confirmed outcomes across relevant versions without erasing role-specific emphasis.
